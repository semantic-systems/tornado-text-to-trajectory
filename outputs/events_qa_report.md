## 3. Linkage

Candidates are generated within 1000 m and ±2 days, then accepted at
gap ≤ 200 m and ≤ 1 day. Linkage uses **geometry and time only**; `event_id`
and `path_guid` are recorded beside each pair so they remain independent checks.

- candidate pairs: **7964**, accepted: **7389**
- polygons matched to a line: **6921 / 9815** (70.5%)
- of those, touching their line (gap = 0 m): **92.9%**
- free-text `event_id` agrees with the spatial assignment on **45.5%** of them

The last number is why linkage is spatial: the string that looks like a join key
disagrees with the geometry on more than half the polygons.

The median unlinked polygon is **135 km** from the nearest line surveyed the

same-day line. Widening the gap threshold cannot recover them. Those rated as a
tornado become hazards of their own in stage 12 (section 5), and most match an
SPC tornado (section 6).

Precision is flat (≈99%) across the grid while recall rises with a wider gap,
which argues for widening. The default stays at 200 m / ±1 day anyway,
because a wrongly attached polygon corrupts a hazard's ground truth while a
missing one only leaves it line-only — and the gold set, being sparse, cannot
observe the failure mode that widening would provoke: two parallel tracks in a
dense outbreak. Users who prefer recall can re-filter `dat_links_2010-01-01_2025-12-31.csv`,
which retains every candidate out to 1000 m.

Read this as a warning, not a reassurance: observed accuracy is ≈100% in *every*
bin, including the lowest. On this gold set `link_confidence` does not
discriminate correct from incorrect links — it is systematically
under-confident, and a low value should not be read as "probably wrong". Two
reasons, both worth keeping in mind: a polygon covering a small slice of a long
track legitimately scores low on containment, and the gold set contains too few
errors (11) to calibrate against. Treat the score as a ranking aid.

### Time-repaired matches (second pass)

The strict rule leaves 1,521 of 11,316 DAT hazards without an SPC track. Most have one within metres whose start differs by a **whole number of hours, to the minute** — the DAT time was saved in the wrong zone or on the wrong date. The second pass links a hazard to an SPC track within 5 km whose start is k hours away, 1 ≤ |k| ≤ 48, to within ±10 min. DAT's times are never changed; the offset is kept on the link (`link_method = time_repaired`, `time_offset_h`, `offset_class`).

**881 more hazards** are linked (86.6% → 94.3% of DAT).

Three checks that do not rest on the time field:

- **The offsets pile up on the hour.** Of 1,192 candidate pairs within the distance, 777 are within 2 min of a whole hour. Pairs that are not one tornado fall anywhere in the hour; their density 20–30 min from it puts chance at ≈ 90 of the 967 candidates kept (9%, an upper bound — it counts candidates, and most hazards with a chance candidate also have a real one).
- **Ratings agree as often as in the first pass.** EF ratings match on 94% of second-pass links, 97% of first-pass links, and 38% for ratings drawn at random; solving (1 − f)·0.966 + f·0.377 = 0.945 puts the share of wrong pairs at **≈ 3.6%**.
- **DAT's own `event_id` sides with SPC.** Where it holds a legible date (50 links), it is SPC's local date and not DAT's for 23, DAT's and not SPC's for 1. For the date repairs alone (25 with a date): SPC 21, DAT 1.

## 7. Limitations to carry into the dataset documentation

1. **Ground truth is a survey estimate.** DAT geometries are drawn by NWS field
   surveyors; coverage varies by office and surveyors cannot reach every
   location. Absence of a polygon is not absence of damage.
2. **The `path_guid` evaluation is conditional** on 289 polygons from
   2017, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, an editing-practice subsample rather than a random one.
3. **29.5% of polygons carry no track line** — mostly by design, but this
   limits how many hazards have both geometry roles (38.4% do).
4. **WFO is missing from DAT on 60.9% of hazards**; stage 16 fills it from Storm Events and the county join, leaving 0.0% without one.
5. **SPC hazards have SPC's straight start→end line**, not a surveyed track; their
   geography is as accurate as the SPC-line check in section 5 says.
6. **The window is fixed at 2010-2025.** DAT is edited retroactively, so a
   later rerun may differ; `retrieved_at` in each manifest pins this snapshot.
