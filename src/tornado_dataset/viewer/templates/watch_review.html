How to review

Labels save as you set them into outputs/annotation/watch_*_sheet.csv; afterwards run python scripts/26_watch_systems.py check. The tabs count labels: one per grouping of each outbreak, one per watch pair.

A · Outbreaks — does each grouping A–I cut the outbreak into the events the record describes?

  1. First, your event list — before looking at any grouping. Open the SPC report pages and the Wikipedia search (panel links) and note each separate event with region and time, e.g. “E1 26 Apr TX–AR evening · E2 27 Apr morning line MS–AL · E3 27 Apr afternoon supercells AL–GA–TN”. Put it in the notes of grouping A. An event = tornadoes from one storm system in one continuous period; two parts are separate if the summaries say so, or if activity stops for several hours and restarts with new storms elsewhere.
  2. Then each grouping (keys a–i): compare the coloured groups with your list. Grey (groups with fewer than 3 tornadoes in the window, ungrouped tornadoes) does not count; dashed tracks lie outside the window — ignore them unless they make a group span two events.
  3. Label:
    1 matcheseach event is mainly one coloured group, and no coloured group holds most of two events
    2 too coarseat least one coloured group holds two events of your list
    3 too fineat least one event is split over two or more coloured groups (typical: at a dashed 12Z line, or along the path)
    4 cannot tellthe summaries do not say whether the parts are one event
    Both errors at once: label the one affecting more tornadoes and note the other.
  4. Helpers: the timeline has one row per group and dashed orange lines at 12Z (the convective-day boundary); hover a row or a group to highlight it, drag on the timeline to move in time. A time window (±3 h) with ▶ shows whether activity is continuous or stops and restarts.

B · Watch pairs — does watch B cover the same storms as watch A?

The linking kept B apart because B lies upstream of (behind) A's storm motion.

  1. The time starts at B's issue time. Filled counties are in effect at the selected time (blue A, orange B); outlines are the polygons, arrows two hours of storm motion; grey = other watches.
  2. Play through with a ±1 h window: do A's storms move into B, or do B's storms form separately behind or beside A's?
  3. Read both SEL texts in the panel: “replaces watch …” or “continues” points to the same threat; “new development”, a second line or another region to a separate one.
  4. Label:
    1 separate systemsB covers other storms — the rejection was right
    2 should linkB covers A's storms, taken over or replaced — the rejection was wrong
    3 cannot tellmap and texts do not settle it

C · Compare — after the outbreak labels

Read-only. Four named groupings of one outbreak on synced maps with one time slider, each with your blind label. Colours follow the reference grouping (r to change it): same colour = same tornadoes together, a new colour = a split, a reference colour gone = a merge. d fades every track that agrees with the reference, leaving only the differences.
